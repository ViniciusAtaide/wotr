# Character Creation
*Source: GameFAQs WotR Guide (80843).*

#### Character Level
Your *character level* is independent from your *class level* since you can choose multiple classes. Increasing your character level requires *experience points* (XP) which you obtain from defeating enemies, completing quests and using your skills. You may rise as high as level 20. The experience point requirement for each character level is as follows:

### Race
Race is an important choice since many choices predispose you to one class over another - for example, elves make great Alchemists but they make substandard Barbarians. Note that some class archetypes are gated by race - for example, only halflings can become Cavaliers of the Paw.

All races besides human have one or two subtypes. If you choose Aasimar, Tiefling or Dhampir, you will also have to choose a Heritage since aasimar and tieflings are the offspring of humans and various planar beings while dhampirs have vampiric ancestry.

#### Human
| Ability Modifiers | +2 any |
| --- | --- |
| Racial Traits | Feat: Bonus feat at 1st level |
| Skilled: +1 point per level to skill of choice |  |
| Exclusive Archetype | Imitator (Slayer) |

Humans are adaptable to any role. The bonus feat of choice gives them a leg up in any possible build. The single +2 attribute means that they may not have as good attributes overall as certain non-human races but their flexibility can make up for it.

#### Elf
| Ability Modifiers | +2 Dexterity, Intelligence; -2 Constitution |
| --- | --- |
| Racial Traits | Keen Senses: +2 bonus to Perception checks |
| Elven Magic: +2 to caster level checks vs. spell resistance; +2 bonus to Knowledge (Arcana) skill checks |  |
| Elven Immunities: Immune to magic sleep effects; +2 save bonus vs. Enchantment |  |
| Elven Weapon Familiarity: Proficient with (composite) longbows, longswords, rapiers and (composite) shortbows; Elven Curve Blade is treated as a martial weapon rather than an exotic weapon |  |
| Exclusive Archetype | Spell Dancer (Magus) |

Compared to humans, elves trade flexibility for some decent but fixed bonuses: an equivalent to Spell Penetration, bonuses to a couple of skills and save bonuses which can help offset a Wisdom deficiency (since Enchantment spells target the Will save). Elven Weapon Familiarity is beneficial to classes that otherwise only receive proficiencies in Simple Weapons.

Half-elves lack the attribute bonuses and other benefits of a pure elf and the flexibility of a human. For what is supposed to be a bit of flavour, the Oblate backgrounds are very powerful. For example, for most classes which are not dependent on Charisma, the *Persuasion* skill is a reason not to dump Charisma. However, making Wisdom the governing attribute means that you can move points from Charisma into Wisdom for bonuses to Perception and Will.

A Martial Disciple gets Improved Unarmed Strike which opens up the Crane Style line of feats without having to pay the full feat tax or take an otherwise-unwanted Monk level.

When a background gives you proficiencies, you will also receive a +1 enhancement bonus with those weapons or armour if you are already proficient in them. If skills granted by a background are already class skills, you will receive a +1 competence bonus.

### Attributes
You begin your career with 10 points in each attribute and a racial bonus that is either +2 to one attribute or +2 to two attributes and -2 to another. You then have 25 points to spend as you see fit.

You will gain one attribute point to spend as you see fit at levels 4, 8, 12, 16 and 20.

#### Point Buys
Not every point is equal. The further away from 10 you get, the greater the cost of the next increment, so that the total cost of taking a given attribute value is as follows:

| Value | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| 7 | +4 |
| 8 | +2 |
| 9 | +1 |
| 10 | - |
| 11 | -1 |
| 12 | -2 |
| 13 | -3 |
| 14 | -5 |
| 15 | -7 |
| 16 | -10 |
| 17 | -13 |
| 18 | -17 |

You can gain additional points to spend in your primary attributes by taking penalties to attributes that are not so important to your character. The minimum attribute value is 7 (or 5 for an attribute for which you have a racial penalty) and the maximum initial value is 18 (or 20 for an attribute for which you have a racial bonus).

If the racial modifier is to two primary attributes, this can give you significantly better attributes than +2 to one attribute of choice. For example, a human Alchemist (primary attributes Intelligence and Dexterity), might start off with the following attributes, assigning +2 to Intelligence:

| 10 | 16 | 12 | 19 | 10 | 10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

However, an elven Alchemist could start with the following attributes for the same point buy:

| 10 | 18 | 10 | 19 | 10 | 10 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Because of the non-linear cost of high attribute values, 18 Dexterity compared to 12 Constitution is equivalent to an additional 5 points to spend at character creation which is a significant benefit. There are other benefits and trade-offs to consider when choosing character race which may make the choice of human (or half-orc, etc.) preferable but attribute spread is something to bear in mind.

An important thing to note is that bonuses accrue on even values above 10 (i.e. 12, 14, 16, etc.) so if you're going to buy one point, you may as well buy two. On the other hand, penalties accrue on odd values below 10 (i.e. 9, 7, 5). Therefore, if you're going to dock one point, you may as well dock two. However, docking *three* is disproportionately harmful since you can't buy a +2 bonus with four points.

That said, there are circumstances under which you may want to select an odd-valued attribute. For example, the Combat Expertise feat has a pre-requisite of 13 Intelligence but Intelligence is of limited use to many combat-focused builds. In this case, it may better to spend the two points elsewhere than take 14 Intelligence. You may also want to select an odd-numbered value for attributes that you are intending to raise with your attribute points gained from levelling up.

#### Dexterity
Dexterity contributes to the following:

- Dodge AC
- Reflex save
- Melee attack rolls (Weapon Finesse)
- Ranged attack rolls (bows, thrown weapons, "ranged touch" attacks)
- Melee damage (Fencing Grace / Slashing Grace / Finesse Training)
- CMD
- CMB (Agile Maneuvers / Fury's Fall)
- Mobility, Stealth and Trickery skills.

**Do you need Strength?**
For the purposes of melee attack and damage, you can use Dexterity but it costs you three feats: Weapon Finesse, Weapon Focus and Fencing / Slashing Grace. You'll need Agile Maneuvers as well if you want to use combat manoeuvres. Do not dump Strength, however. Otherwise you'll be burdened easily and lose all your Dexterity benefits. You'll also want to be able to unlock the necessary feats by level 2; level 3 (when Rogues get Dexterity to damage for free) is quite a long time to be doing scratch damage. Besides taking a while to get going, Dexterity builds are married to a single weapon type and have fewer ways to increase their damage output.

Do not take a Strength penalty if you are creating a bow user since you'll do reduced damage with each arrow.

#### Constitution
Constitution contributes to the following:

- +HP per level
- Fortitude save.

Constitution doesn't contribute to very much. Bonus HP are good but since "HP tanking" isn't really a viable option in this game, you don't need to go overboard. If you have slots going spare, you can replicate Constitution bonuses with feats: Toughness and (Improved) Great Fortitude. However, since Fortitude saves are a thing and the consequences of failure are typically severe, you don't want to take a penalty.

#### Intelligence
Intelligence contributes to the following:

- Skill points per level
- AC (Canny Defense)
- Knowledge skills.

Skills are useful which makes Intelligence useful on any character. 14 is sufficient to grant a bonus skill point per level. Canny Defense is a class ability for Sword Saints and Duelists and adds your Intelligence modifier to dodge AC.

#### Wisdom
Wisdom contributes to the following:

- AC and CMD (Monk and Instinctual Warrior, no armour, no shield)
- Will save
- Lore and Perception skills.

Another relatively unimportant stat for non-spellcasters. The AC bonus is a compelling reason to take a single Monk level. The Will save is a reason not to make this a dump stat although (Improved) Iron Will can make up for a deficiency.

#### Charisma
Charisma contributes to the following:

- AC and CMD (Scaled Fist, no armour, no shield)
- Saving throws (Divine Favor Paladin feat)
- Persuasion and Use Magic Device skills.

Charisma would be a dump stat were it not for the Persuasion skill tied to it. Persuasion is a good reason to choose a Charisma-based class for your PC and if your chosen class does not have Persuasion as a class skill, it may be worth taking a Skill Focus feat.

### Skills
Skills are a way to interact with the world outside of combat. They are driven off various attributes and different classes have differing access, some classes being skilled, others not so. Some classes are inherently more suited to various skills - these are "class skills". Investing a point in a class skill grants a one-off +3 bonus to checks against that skill. You additionally apply relevant attribute modifiers to skill checks. For example, 14 Charisma will add +2 to Persuasion checks while 8 Charisma will impose a -1 penalty.

High Intelligence grants bonus skills equal to one point per two character levels for each point of Intelligence bonus. This means that a character with 12 Intelligence would gain a bonus skill point on each odd-numbered level while one with 14 Intelligence would gain a point on every level (and 18 Intelligence would grant two extra skill points per character level). The "Skilled" trait of humans and half-orcs is equivalent to +4 Intelligence for most builds where Intelligence isn't a primary class attribute.

##### Enhanced Attacks
A companion's attacks are treated as magical for the purpose of overcoming damage reduction. For every 3HD the companion has, it gains a +1 enhancement bonus on attack rolls and damage and a +1 resistance bonus to saving throws.

##### Devotion
A companion gains a +4 morale bonus on Will saves vs. enchantment spells and effects.

##### Multiattack
If a companion has at least three attacks, attacks with secondary weapons are made at a -2 AB penalty rather than -5.

##### Bleeding Strike
If the first attack in a round succeeds, the enemy bleeds for an additional 1d6 damage each round. The Aggressor gains +1 bonus to attack and damage rolls against an affected enemy. This bonus increases by an additional +1 at levels 10, 15 and 20.

##### Bullying Thrill
Whenever the companion succeeds at a bull rush, overrun or trip combat maneuver check, it gains a +2 morale bonus to attack and damage rolls until the end of its next turn.

##### Artful Acrobat
The companion gains a competence bonus to Mobility checks equal to half its level.

##### Devil May Care
The companion can't be flanked.

##### One Foot in the Grave
The companion gains a +4 morale bonus to Fortitude saves against effects that could not normally affect undead.

##### Fast Movement
The companion's speed is 10' greater than normal when wearing no armour and carrying a light load. This increases to 20' at 6th level.

##### Destructive Wrecker
The companion's natural attacks ignore an inanimate object's DR and count as adamantine for the purposes of overcoming a construct's DR.

At 6th level, you may bestow Divine Fortune upon yourself as a standard action once per day. For a number of rounds equal to half your class level, you make all rolls twice, taking the better result each time. This increases to twice per day at 12th level and three times at 18th level.

##### Madness Domain
You can use Vision of Madness (3 + Wisdom modifier) times per day as a melee touch attack. The recipient receives a bonus equal to half your class level on one type of roll (attacks, saves, skills) and a penalty to the other two. At 8th level you can use Aura of Madness. All enemies within 30' must save each round (DC10 + half class level + Wisdom modifier) or become confused. You can maintain the aura for a number of rounds per day equal to your class level.

You can do this twice per day at 12th level, three times at 16th level and four times at 20th level.

##### Protection Domain
You receive a +1 resistance bonus to all saving throws. This increases by +1 for each five class levels. All allies within 30' receive a +1 deflection bonus to AC and 5 resistance to all elements. You can maintain the aura for a number of rounds per day equal to your class level.
