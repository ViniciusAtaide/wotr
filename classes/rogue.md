# Rogue
*Source: GameFAQs WotR Guide (80843).*

The Rogue has the following characteristics:

- Primary attribute: Dexterity
- Medium (3/4) AB progression
- 8 HP at level 1, then 5 HP per level (d8 hit die)
- High saving throws for Reflex, low saving throws for Fortitude and Will
- 5 skill points per level.

Class Skills: Athletics, Mobility, Trickery, Stealth, Knowledge (World), Perception, Persuasion, Use Magic Device

#### Rogue Proficiencies
Rogues are proficient with all simple weapons, rapier, short sword and short bow. They are proficient with light armor but not with shields.

#### Trapfinding
A Rogue adds half her class level on Perception checks.

#### Sneak Attack
A Rogue deals extra damage when her opponent is denied Dexterity bonus or when she flanks her opponent. She does an additional 1d6 damage, increasing by 1d6 every odd-numbered class level.

#### Rogue Talents
An Eldritch Scoundrel can select (Improved) Uncanny Dodge as Rogue Talents.

The Rogue may select from the following talents:

##### Weapon Focus
This is the feat of the same name.

##### Canny Observer
+4 to Perception checks.

##### Combat Trick
Any combat feat that the character qualifies for.

##### Emboldening Strike
When doing sneak attack damage, gain a +1 circumstance bonus to saving throws for every 2 sneak attack dice rolled for 1 round.

##### Fast Stealth
Move at full speed when using the Stealth skill.

##### Focusing Attack - Confused
If you are confused, performing an attack that does sneak damage will bring you to your senses.

##### Focusing Attack - Shaken
If you are shaken, performing an attack that does sneak damage will snap you out of it.

##### Focusing Attack - Sickened
If you are sickened, performing an attack that does sneak damage will enable you to recover.

##### Intimidating Prowess
This is the feat of the same name.

##### Iron Guts
+1 to saves vs. poisons and +4 bonus to saves vs. effects that cause the character to be sickened or nauseated.

##### Jump Up
You do not provoke attacks of opportunity when standing up from prone.

##### Mystery Seeker
Add Knowledge (Arcana) to your list of class skills.

##### Skill Focus
This is the feat of the same name.

##### Slow Reactions
Opponents damaged by the character's sneak attack cannot make attacks of opportunity for 1 round.

##### Survivalist
Add Lore (Nature) to your list of class skills.

##### Terrain Mastery
The character gains a favored terrain like the Ranger class feature but the bonus does not increase with level.

##### Trailblazer
Gain immunity to difficult terrain.

##### Unbalancing Trick
Gain Trip as a bonus feat.

##### Underhanded Trick
Gain Dirty Trick as a bonus feat.

##### Weakening Wound
When making a sneak attack against a creature with damage reduction, the target's DR is reduced by an amount equal to the character's level for 2 rounds.

#### Advanced Rogue Talents
These unlock at 10th level.

##### Blinding Strike
Prerequisites:
Character level: 15

Gain Blinding Critical as a bonus feat.

##### Confounding Blades
Prerequisites:
Slow Reactions

A creature struck with a melee weapon that does sneak attack damage cannot make attacks of opportunity for 2-5 rounds.

##### Crippling Strike
An opponent damaged by a sneak attack takes 2 points of Strength damage.

##### Dispelling Attack
A sneak attack casts a targeted Dispel Magic affecting one spell active on the target. Caster level is equal to character level.

##### Double Debilitation
Prerequisites:
Debilitating Injury

When using Debilitating Injury, select two penalties to inflict.

##### Familiar
The rogue gains a familiar as in a Wizard's arcane bond.

##### Hunter's Surprise
Once per day, you may designate an adjacent enemy. Until the end of your next turn, your attacks against that enemy add sneak damage, even if the enemy is not flanked or flat-footed.

##### Improved Evasion
You take no damage on a successful Reflex save and only half damage from a failed save.

##### Opportunist
Make an attack of opportunity against an opponent struck by another character. This ability can only trigger once per round.

##### Petrifying Strike
An opponent damaged by a sneak attack takes 2 points of Dexterity damage.

##### Skill Mastery (Knowledge)
Take 10 when making Knowledge (Arcana) or Knowledge (World) checks.

##### Skill Mastery (Lore)
Take 10 when making Lore (Nature) or Lore (Religion) checks.

##### Skill Mastery (Stealth and Trickery)
Take 10 when making Stealth or Trickery checks.

##### Slippery Mind
Add your Dexterity modifier to saves vs. mind-affecting conditions.

##### Weapon Snatcher
Make a Trickery check rather than a combat maneuver check when trying to disarm an opponent.

##### Wearying Strike
An enemy taking sneak attack damage also suffers 1 point of Constitution damage.

#### Evasion
From 2nd level, if a Rogue makes a Reflex save against an attack that normally deals half damage on a successful save, she instead takes no damage.

#### Danger Sense
At 3rd level, a Rogue gains a +1 bonus on Reflex saves to avoid traps and a +1 dodge bonus to AC against attacks made by traps. In addition, she gains a +1 bonus on Perception checks to avoid being surprised by a foe. These bonuses increase by +1 every 3 levels (maximum +6 at 18th level).

#### Finesse Training
At 3rd level, a Rogue may select a finessable weapon that she has proficiency in. When she attacks with this weapon, she adds her Dexterity modifier instead of her Strength modifier to the damage roll. She can select a second weapon at 11th level and a third at 19th level.

The weapons selectable are: Bite, Claw, Dagger, Dueling Sword, Elven Curve Blade, Estoc, Handaxe, Kama, Kukri, Light Hammer, Light Mace, Light Pick, Light Shield, Nunchaku, Punching Dagger, Rapier, Sai, Shortsword, Sickle, Starknife, Unarmed.

Finesse Training is the only way for a Dexterity-based character to add damage to the Elven Curve Blade or any sort of bludgeoning weapon.

#### Debilitating Injury
At 4th level, when a Rogue deals sneak attack damage, she may also inflict a debuff for 1 round:

- **Bewildered**
The opponent takes a -2 penalty to AC and a further -2 penalty to attacks made by the Rogue. At 10th and 16th levels the penalty to AC against the Rogue's attacks increases by -2 (to a maximum of -8).
- **Disoriented**
The opponent takes a -2 penalty to attack rolls and a further -2 penalty to attacks made against the Rogue. At 10th and 16th levels the penalty to attack rolls made against the Rogue increases by -2 (to a maximum of -8).
- **Hampered**
The target's speed is reduced by half.

#### Uncanny Dodge
At 4th level, a Rogue cannot be caught flat-footed or lose her Dexterity bonus to AC if the attacker is invisible.

#### Improved Uncanny Dodge
At 8th level, a Rogue cannot be flanked. An enemy Rogue cannot hit you with a sneak attack unless she has at least four more Rogue levels than you.

#### Master Strike
At 20th level, when a Rogue deals sneak attack damage, she can kill the opponent outright if it fails a Fortitude save (DC 20 + Dexterity modifier). If the target makes its save, it becomes immune to the effect.

##### Playing a Rogue
A Rogue specializes in precision damage. A Rogue, or one of its archetypes, is the easiest path to applying dexterity to damage since they get to apply it to three weapon types. Because of Finesse Training, Rogues are the only class able to add dexterity to damage while dual-wielding. However, there are a number of shortcomings not least of which is weak saves for both Fortitude and Will which typically come with more severe consequences than failing a Reflex save. The greatest shortcoming, however, is that there are a number of classes that can do what a Rogue does and a whole lot more, such as a Slayer, a Vivisectionist or even a Shadow Shaman.

A Dark Lurker trades Evasion for a powerful ability against larger creatures and you may want to play a gnome or halfling to take best advantage of this. She also gains the Blind Fight line of feats but each one is received in place of a rogue talent which *could* be the corresponding feat but which could also be anything else.

An Eldritch Scoundrel trades a lot of the Rogue's power for spellcasting. Since they gain access to the full wizard spell list, this is not bad. You can make up deficient defenses with Mage Armor (and later Archmage Armor), Shield, Mirror Image and so forth. Sense Vitals makes up for some of the reduced sneak dice. I would not recommend going into Arcane Trickster because that hurts BAB without giving enough in return.

A Knife Master has a USP of gaining more powerful sneak attacks with a specific set of weapons. An extra 1 damage per sneak die can amount to as much as 50 points of damage per round.

The Master of All has a reputation of being one of the true stinker classes. However, this is unfair. If you're thinking of playing a Rogue in the first place, you're already committed to being a mid-level damage dealer with utility. Skills are useful and make the game more pleasant. A Master of All trades 2d6 sneak damage (you'd take Accomplished Sneak Attacker, after all) for - effectively - 58 skill points, all-in. That's actually a good trade and is something that you won't be getting from a Slayer, Vivisectionist or Shadow Shaman.

A Rowdy trades subtlety for the Vital Strike line of feats and unlocks these much earlier than other classes: a Fighter won't be getting Greater Vital Strike until 16th level at least. You can boost this with as much as 70 points of precision damage on top of what are already damaging hits. You'll want to use a reach weapon (Glaive or Fauchard) to get around the problem of compromised defense since you will not be putting points into Dexterity. Also, be aware that a Two-Handed Fighter built for Vital Strike will do more damage than a Rowdy.

A Sylvan Trickster doesn't pay too dearly for access to hexes, although Resist Nature's Lure instead of Uncanny Dodge is a horrible trade. The problem is one of synergy. A Rogue is an attacker, not a caster, and spending a round casting, say, Evil Eye is perhaps not the best use of her time. It is a decent dip to unlock Iceplant.

The Thug is good (for 3 or 4 levels at least) for melee characters looking to improve their intimidation skills. This is another archetype where Strength is more important than Dexterity since you'd want to build for Power Attack, Cornugon Smash, Intimidating Prowess and Dreadful Carnage. Note that frightening enemies is a double-edged sword, since enemies running away prolongs encounters and there is no way to turn the ability off. If you don't expect to finish your opponent off in a single round of attacks, the ability to sacrifice 1d6 sneak damage to inflict the sickened status is outstanding.

Finally, the Underground Chemist is another class with a bad reputation and this *is* justified. The archetype abilities are worse than the abilities of the base class (after all, you probably wouldn't *choose* Precise Splash Weapons were it a normal Rogue Talent and you generally have something better to do than chuck a flask). If the discoveries are what appeal to you, a Vivisectionist does everything better.

#### Blade from the Shadows
From 2nd level, and when within 5' of a larger creature, the Dark Lurker can use her standard action to step into the creature's shadow and emerge from the shadow of another larger enemy within 30' and deliver a sneak attack with a melee weapon. At nighttime, the Lurker gains a +2 circumstance bonus to the attack roll.

Additionally, at nighttime, the Dark Lurker gains a +2 bonus to initiative rolls.

#### Blade from the Shadows (Full Attack)
From 14th level, when using the Blade from the Shadiows ability, the Dark Lurker can make a full round attack with a melee weapon. The first attack is a sneak attack and the Lurker receives a +2 circumstance bonus to the attack roll at nighttime.

#### Instinctual Sense
At 20th level, a Dark Lurker gains blindsight with a range of 30'.

Whenever a Thug uses Persuasion to demoralize a creature, the duration of the shaken condition is increased by 1 round. If the target is shaken for 4 or more rounds, it is additionally frightened for 1 round.

#### Brutal Beating
At 3rd level, when the Thug deals sneak attack damage, she can forego 1d6 of damage to make the target sickened for a number of rounds equal to half her class level. This ability does not stack - only the most recent duration applies.

### Underground Chemist
An Underground Chemist is a cut-price alchemist.

- Can throw acid flasks and the like
- Loses Evasion.

#### Chemical Weapons
At 2nd level, an Underground Chemist gains the Throw Anything feat and adds her Intelligence modifier to damage dealt with splash weapons.

#### Precise Splash Weapons
At 4th level, an Underground Chemist can deal sneak attack damage with splash weapons.

#### Discovery
At 10th level, an Underground Chemist unlocks certain discoveries that he can take in place of Rogue Talents: Enhance Potion, Extend Potion, Nauseating Flesh, Preserve Organs, Spontaneous Healing and Mummification (requires Preserve Organs).
