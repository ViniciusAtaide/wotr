# Slayer
*Source: GameFAQs WotR Guide (80843).*

The Slayer has the following characteristics:

- Primary attributes: Strength, Dexterity and Constitution
- Full AB progression
- 10 HP at level 1, then 6 HP per level (d10 hit die)
- High saving throws for Fortitude and Reflex, low saving throws for Will
- Gains sneak attacks
- 3 skill points per level.

Class Skills: Athletics, Mobility, Stealth, Knowledge (World), Lore (Nature), Perception, Persuasion

#### Slayer Proficiencies
Slayers are proficient with all simple and martial weapons, light and medium armor and shields.

#### Studied Target
The Slayer can study a target as a move action, gaining a +1 bonus on weapon attacks and damage rolls against it as well as +1 to DCs of any Slayer class abilities. At 5th, 10th, 15th and 20th levels, the bonuses increase by +1 (maximum +5).

#### Slayer Talents
From 2nd level, the Slayer may select abilities from Rogue Talents, counting his Slayer class level as his Rogue level. Alternatively, he may choose from a Ranger's Combat Style Feats. He expands the Rogue Talent list with two additional abilities:

##### Trapfinding
The Slayer gains Trickery as a class skill along with the Rogue's Trapfinding ability, using his class level as his effective Rogue level.

##### Slayer Camouflage
Requires Terrain Mastery and Advanced Talents. A Slayer can use the Stealth skill to hide in a favored terrain even if the terrain doesn't grant cover.

Although the slayer can choose from Combat Style Feats, he can only do so three times in total and at certain levels: 2 or 4, 6 or 8 and 10 or greater.

#### Sneak Attack
From 3rd level, the Slayer deals extra damage when his opponent is denied Dexterity bonus or when he flanks his opponent. He does an additional 1d6 damage, increasing by 1d6 every three additional class levels (maximum 6d6 at 18th level). After doing sneak damage, the Slayer is able to apply Studied Target bonuses to the opponent.

#### Studied Target (Swift)
At 7th level, the Slayer can study an opponent as a swift action.

#### Advanced Talents
At 10th level, the Slayer may select from the Advanced Rogue Talents.

#### Slayer's Advance
At 13th level, once per day, the Slayer can increase his movement speed by 30' for 1 round and move freely without provoking attacks of opportunity. At 17th level, he can do this twice per day.

#### Quarry
At 14th level, the Slayer can designate a target as his quarry as a standard action. He receives a +2 insight bonus to attack rolls made against the quarry and all critical hits are automatically confirmed. Only one creature at a time may be designated as a quarry.

#### Improved Quarry
At 19th level, the Slayer may designate a quarry as a free action and the insight bonus to attack rolls increases to +4.

#### Master Slayer
At 20th level, the Slayer can make an attack against a studied target as a standard action at full bonus. If the attack hits, the target must make a Fortitude save (DC 20 + Intelligence modifier) or die. The Slayer can use this ability against a given opponent only once.

##### Playing a Slayer
The Slayer is a reliable damage dealer with full BAB and sneak dice. It is also a very feat-rich class so you can build one any way you want. In particular, combat styles give easy access to feat-intensive things like two weapon fighting or shield bashing without requiring a heavy investment in Dexterity.

Studied Target is the class's superpower. Up to +5 untyped attack and damage is as good as a Fighter's weapon training and you can boost this further through the Quarry abilities: +9 AB, +5 damage and automatic critical confirmation is pretty good. For purposes of action economy, it is good to be able to apply these as swift actions which suggests that you really want to take 19 levels. The 20th level gives you the Master Slayer ability. Most capstone instakill abilities are rubbish because the DCs are quite low. However, Studied Target will give +5 to the DC so it has a rather better chance of sticking.

The Arcane Enforcer is an interesting variation. You pay a lot for access to exploits but there is some synergy: Dimensional Slide is good for any character and Armored Mask can be used to open up the Archmage Armor mythic ability. Wooden Flesh (+2 AC and a whole raft of immunities) is one of the strongest abilities in the game. For a Greater Exploit, spell resistance is probably the only thing worthwhile.

A Bloodseeker trades access to Talents and Advanced Talents for not enough. He gets bonuses for killing enemies but will be worse at doing that than a regular Slayer with access to combat style feats. There are some decent Blood Pool abilities (the level 6 ones in particular) and the capstone ability, Master of Blood, is much better than Master Slayer. However, most of the abilities are weak spells and one hopes that a 14th level Slayer would do more damage simply attacking an enemy than casting *Wracking Ray*. Even if you pump points in Intelligence rather than Strength and Dexterity, a Bloodseeker makes a poor DC caster.

The Deliverer (eventually) becomes powerful with the right alignment choice. Unfortunately, the right alignment choice is probably Lawful Good which creates a terrible RP stench. The +2d6 damage comes at the cost of two Slayer Talents. This wouldn't be so bad if you didn't miss out on the 2nd and 10th level talents since those are levels when Combat Style feats come online.

An Executioner trades a talent and the useful Quarry ability for a couple of rubbish abilities and an interesting one. The Assassinate ability is tricky to pull off (and requires serious investment in Stealth) but delivers Coup de Grace which is always strong, rather than an instakill with a DC.

The Imitator is a pretty terrible class choice. The Imitation ability is so-so but the cost is huge: three talents, half your sneak damage and Studied Target. When an archetype loses the class superpower, you know it's a stinker. If you want to play a fighter or a rogue, play a fighter or a rogue.

The Spawn Slayer gains advantages over the largest monsters but at the cost of not being able to use Studied Target as a Swift Action. In this game, most significant enemies are large or larger so you end up in credit.

I don't know what the Stygian Slayer is trying to do. You give up the 4th level talent for an Invisibility spell. Not terrible (other than True Seeing being commonplace). However, losing the 10th level talent for an ability where you become a cloud of gas which is *unable to attack* for a minute at a time is really quite awful.

The Vanguard gives up two lots of Slayer Talents for a teamwork feat (which at 2nd level is not going to be Outflank) and the rather rubbish Vanguard's Bond. He does get Uncanny Dodge which is OK.

### Arcane Enforcer
An Arcane Enforcer knows enough magic to use it to hunt down his target.

- High saving throws for Reflex and Will, low saving throws for Fortitude
- Can use Arcanist Exploits
- Loses Quarry and three Slayer Talents
- Class Skills: Athletics, Mobility, Stealth, Knowledge (Arcana), Knowledge (World), Perception, Use Magic Device.

#### Arcane Reservoir
An Arcane Enforcer has a reservoir of magical energy that is refreshed daily. Its maximum capacity is his class level +3.

#### Arcanist Exploits
An Arcane Enforcer can choose an Arcanist Exploit at level 1 and every 3 subsequent levels. From 13th level, he gains access to Greater Exploits.

#### Bloodseeker's Cursed Lineage
A Bloodseeker is treated as undead for the purposes of channel energy effects.

#### Blood Pool
At 2nd level, a Bloodseeker gains a Blood Pool which fuels his abilities. It initially contains 2 points and increases by +1 for each additional class level. The DC of Blood Pool abilities is 10 + class level / 2 + INT modifier.

#### Bloodlust
From 2nd level, a Bloodseeker gains a +1 bonus to attack and damage rolls for each enemy slain which lasts until the end of combat. The total bonus cannot exceed half the Bloodseeker's Intelligence modifier.

#### Sharp Teeth
At 2nd level, a Bloodseeker gains a secondary 1d6 bite attack (1d4) if small. When confirming a critical hit against a living target, his bite attack replenishes 3 points of his Blood Pool.

#### Master of Blood

#### Determined Zeal
When a Deliverer attacks an opponent whose alignment is two steps away from his own (for example, a Lawful Evil Deliverer fighting a Chaotic Evil cultist), he gains a +2 bonus to Will saves against the opponent's abilities as well as the benefit of the Diehard feat until the end of his next turn.

#### Divine Anathema
At 10th level, when a Deliverer's studied target's alignment is two steps away from his own, the Deliverer's attacks deal an additional 2d6 damage.

#### Focused Killer
The attack, damage and DC bonuses from Studied Target are increased by an additional +1 vs. humanoid targets and decreased by -1 against non-humanoid targets.

#### Painful Strike
A creature that takes sneak damage must make a Fortitude save (DC = 10 + class level / 2 + Intelligence modifier) or become sickened for 1d4 rounds.

#### Assassinate
At 10th level and outside combat, an Executioner may deliver a Coup de Grace attack to a studied target. At 14th level, when an Executioner assassinates a target, enemies within 30' become demoralized. At 19th level, an enemy surviving an assassinate attempt takes 2d6 bleed damage.

#### Imitation
As a free action, an Imitator can adopt Fighter, Barbarian, Monk or Rogue stance for 1 minute. He can use this ability a number of times per day equal to half his class level + his Intelligence modifier.

##### Fighter Stance
The Imitator gains +1 to attack and damage rolls, increasing by +1 every five class levels (max +5 at 20th level). At 6th and 14th levels, he gains the benefits of Armor Training.

##### Barbarian Stance
The Imitator gains temporary HP equal to his class level. He gains Uncanny Dodge at 4th level and DR 1/- at 7th level. The damage reduction increases by +1 every three additional class levels (max 5/- at 19th level).

##### Monk Stance
The Imitator gains Improved Unarmed Strike. At 2nd level, while unarmored, he adds half his Intelligence modifier to his AC. At 4th level, he gains Evasion. At 8th level, his unarmed attacks count as magic and cold iron for the purposes of overcoming damage reduction. At 11th level, he gains an extra unarmed attack while unarmored and a second additional attack at 20th level.

##### Rogue Stance
The Imitator gains 1d6 sneak attack plus 1d6 every additional three class levels (max 7d6 at 19th level). He gains Evasion at 4th level and Uncanny Dodge at 8th level.

#### Sneak Attack (Imitator)
An Imitator gains +1d6 sneak damage at 3rd level and an additional +1d6 at 9th and 15th levels.

#### Studied Spawn
At 5th level, if the target is Large (or bigger), the studied target bonuses increase by +1. At 10th level, if the target is Huge, the bonuses increase by +2. At 15th level, the bonuses are Gargantuan: +3 and at 20th level the bonuses are Colossal: +4.

#### Gain Leverage
At 7th level, the Spawn Slayer gains +1 CMB and CMD advantages against studied targets which are Large or bigger. This increases quadratically with the opponent's size (Huge: +2, Gargantuan: +4 and Colossal: +8).

#### Teamwork Feat
At 2nd level, the Slayer may select any teamwork feat for which he meets the prerequisites.

#### Vanguard Tactician
At 2nd level, once per day the Vanguard may grant any teamwork feat he possesses to all allies within 30' for 3 rounds + half his class level. At higher levels, the Vanguard may choose an additional use of this ability over a Slayer talent.

#### Vanguard's Bond
At 4th level, as a move action, the Vanguard may grant half his studied target bonus to all allies within 30' for a number of rounds equal to his Intelligence modifier.

#### Uncanny Dodge
At 7th level, the Vanguard cannot be caught flat-footed or lose his Dexterity bonus to AC if the attacker is invisible. If you gain this ability twice from another class, you gain Improved Uncanny Dodge instead.
