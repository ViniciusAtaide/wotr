# Bloodrager
*Source: GameFAQs WotR Guide (80843).*

The Bloodrager has the following characteristics:

- Primary attributes: Strength, Constitution and Charisma
- Full AB progression
- 8 HP per level
- High saving throws for Fortitude, low saving throws for Reflex and Will
- Bloodrage ability
- Caster level progression from level 4
- May cast arcane spells up to 4th-level
- Spells are cast spontaneously
- Spellcasting attribute is Charisma
- 3 skill points per level.

Class Skills: Athletics, Mobility, Knowledge (Arcana), Lore (Nature), Perception, Persuasion

Independent of bloodline, a Bloodrager has the same proficiencies as a Barbarian. A Bloodrager shares a number of other abilities with a Barbarian as well: Fast Movement, (Improved) Uncanny Dodge, Damage Reduction and Indomitable Will.

#### Bloodrage
At 1st level a Bloodrager can rage for a number of rounds per day equal to 4 + his Constitution modifier. For each class level after 1st, he can rage for 2 additional rounds per day. Bloodrage is a free action.

When enraged, the Bloodrager gains +2 to attack rolls, melee damage rolls, thrown weapon damage rolls and Will saving throws along with a -2 penalty to AC. He also gains 2 temporary HP per hit die. Ending the rage is a free action and the Bloodrager is fatigued for 1 minute.

#### Blood Sanctuary
At 3rd level, a Bloodrager gains a +2 bonus on saving throws against spells cast by himself or an ally.

#### Bloodline Feat
At 6th, 9th, 12th, 15th and 18th levels, a Bloodrager may select a bonus feat from the list available for his his chosen bloodline.

Note that you must meet the prerequisites for bonus feats so that *Dodge*, for example, requires Dexterity 13. If you select the Second Bloodrager Bloodline mythic ability, you can choose two feats, one from each bloodline.

#### Bloodline Spell
At 7th, 10th, 13th and 16th levels, a Bloodrager receives a additional known spell from his bloodline.

#### Greater Bloodrage
At 11th level, the bonuses the Bloodrager gains from raging increase to +3 and he gains +3 temporary HP per hit die. When entering a rage, the Bloodrager may cast a 2nd level spell or lower with "Touch" or "Self" range upon himself as a swift action.

#### Tireless Bloodrage
At 17th level, the Bloodrager no longer becomes fatigued after raging.

#### Mighty Bloodrage
At 20th level, the bonuses the Bloodrager gains from raging increase to +4 and he gains +4 temporary HP per hit die. On entering rage, he may cast a spell of any level upon himself.

##### Bonus Feats (Abyssal)
The Bloodrager may choose from the following feats: Bull Rush, Cleave, Great Fortitude, Intimidating Prowess, Power Attack, Sunder Armor and Toughness.

##### Claws (Abyssal)
You grow claws when enraged. The claws do 1d6 damage (1d4 if small). At 4th level, the claws are considered magic weapons for overcoming damage resistance. At 8th level, the damage increases to 1d8 (1d6). At 12th level, the claws become flaming weapons, dealing an additional 1d6 fire damage.

##### Demonic Bulk
At 4th level, when entering bloodrage, you grow one size category larger than your base size.

##### Demon Resistances
At 8th level, you gain resistance 5 to acid, cold and fire. At 16th level, the resistances increase to 10.

##### Abyssal Bloodrage
At 12th level, the morale bonuses to attack and damage granted by bloodrage increase by +1, but the AC penalty becomes -4. At 16th and 20th levels, the bonuses increase by an additional +1.

##### Demonic Aura
At 16th level, when raging, you can create a 5' radius aura which inflicts 2d6 fire damage + your Constitution modifier to creatures that end their turns within it.

##### Demonic Immunities
At 20th level, you become immune to electricity and poison.

##### Bonus Feats (Arcane)
The Bloodrager may choose from the following feats: Arcane Strike, Combat Casting, Combat Reflexes, Disruptive, Improved Initiative, Iron Will and Power Attack.

##### Disruptive Bloodrage
While raging, enemies within 10' of you have a -2 penalty on concentration checks.

##### Arcane Bloodrage
At 4th level, when entering bloodrage, you can apply one of the following spells to yourself: *Blur*, *Protection from Arrows* or *Resist Energy*.

##### Greater Arcane Bloodrage
At 8th level, when entering bloodrage, you can apply one of the following spells to yourself: *Haste* or *Displacement*.

##### Caster's Scourge
At 12th level, enemy casters within 10' must make a concentration check with a DC of 15 + spell level x 2.

##### True Arcane Bloodrage
At 16th level, when entering bloodrage, you can apply one of the following spells to yourself: *Beast Shape IV*, *Dragonkind I* or *Transformation*.

##### Caster's Bane
At 20th level, spellcasters within 10' automatically fail attempts at casting defensively.

##### Bonus Feats (Draconic)
The Bloodrager may choose from the following feats: Blind Fight, Cleave, Great Fortitude, Improved Initiative, Intimidating Prowess, Power Attack and Toughness.

##### Claws (Draconic)
You grow claws when enraged. The claws do 1d6 damage (1d4 if small). At 4th level, the claws are considered magic weapons for overcoming damage resistance. At 8th level, the damage increases to 1d8 (1d6). At 12th level, the claws become flaming weapons, dealing an additional 1d6 damage of your bloodline's energy type.

##### Draconic Resistance
At 4th level, you gain resistance 5 against your bloodline's energy type and a +1 natural armour bonus to AC. At 8th level, resistance increases to 10 and the armour bonus to +2. At 16th level, your natural armour bonus increases to +4.

##### Breath Weapon
At 8th level, you gain a breath weapon that you can use once per day. This does 1d6 energy damage per class level to creatures caught in the area of effect. Affected creatures can attempt a reflex save (DC = 10 + class level / 2 + Constitution modifier). The shape of the breath weapon depends on the chosen dragon type. At 16th level, you can use this breath weapon twice per day and at 20th level, three times.

##### Dragon Wings
At 12th level, you gain wings during bloodrage.

##### Dragon Form
At 16th level, when entering a bloodrage, you can choose to take the form of your chosen dragon type as if using *Dragonkind II*.

##### Power of Wyrms
At 20th level, you gain immunity to paralysis, sleep and damage from your bloodline's energy type. You also gain blindsense 60'.

The *Burning Hands* and *Scorching Ray* spells that are unlocked through the bloodline actually do damage corresponding to the bloodline's element.

##### Bonus Feats (Elemental)
The Bloodrager may choose from the following feats: Cleave, Dodge, Great Fortitude, Improved Initiative, Lightning Reflexes, Power Attack and Weapon Focus.

##### Elemental Strikes
As a swift action, you can imbue your melee attacks with elemental energy for 1 round, adding 1d6 points of energy damage. You can use this ability three times per day. At 8th level, you can use this ability five times per day. At 20th level, all your melee attacks do this damage and you do not need to activate it.

##### Elemental Resistance
At 4th level, you gain resistance 10 against your bloodline's energy type.

##### Elemental Movement
At 8th level, your base speed increases by 10' and you gain a +2 bonus to saving throws against spells and abilities with your elemental descriptor.

##### Power of the Elements
At 12th level, the damage from your Elemental Strikes ability increases to 2d6 and the DC of spells with your elemental descriptor increases by +2.

##### Elemental Form
At 16th level, once per day when entering bloodrage, you can assume the form of an elemental as if using *Elemental Body IV*.

##### Elemental Body
At 20th level you gain immunity to sneak attacks, critical hits and your bloodline's element.

##### Bonus Feats (Fey)
The Bloodrager may choose from the following feats: Combat Mobility, Combat Reflexes, Dodge, Improved Initiative, Intimidating Prowess, Lightning Reflexes and Improved Lightning Reflexes.

##### Confusing Critical
When you confirm a critical hit, the target must succeed at a Will saving throw or be confused for 1 round (DC = 10 + class level / 2 + Constitution modifier). This is a mind-affecting compulsion effect for purposes of determining immunity.

##### Leaping Charger
At 4th level, you ignore difficult terrain.

##### Blurring Movement
At 8th level, when moving 10' or more, you gain the effects of the *Blur* spell for 1 round.

##### Quickling Bloodrage
At 12th level, you gain the benefits of *Haste* while enraged.

##### One with Nature
At 16th level, plant and animal enemy types within 15' must make a Will save (DC = 10 + class level / 2 + Charisma modifier) or become fascinated.

##### Fury of the Fey
At 20th level, while raging, all your melee attacks are considered to have the Bane enchantment. This ability does not stack with the *actual* Bane enchantment.

##### Bonus Feats (Hag)
The Bloodrager may choose from the following feats: Blind Fight, Improved Blind Fight, Greater Blind Fight, Deceitful, Great Fortitude, Improved Great Fortitude and Intimidating Prowess.

##### Wicked Gaze
As a standard action, you can target an enemy within 30' and inflict a -2 penalty to attack rolls and AC for 1 round / class level. A successful Will save (DC = 10 + class level / 2 + Charisma modifier) reduces the duration to 1 round. At 8th level, the penalty increases to -4. You can use this ability a number of times per day equal to 3 + your Charisma modifier.

##### Hag Fortitude
At 4th level, you become immune to disease and at 8th level you become immune to poison.

##### Hag Transformation
From 12th level, while bloodraging, you gain the Scent ability and a +2 bonus to Intimidate checks. Your rage bonuses to attack and damage rolls increase by +1 but you suffer a -2 penalty to Dexterity and Wisdom. From 16th level, you apply the bonus to Will saves to Fortitude and Reflex saves as well.

##### Covenguard
From 16th level, you can cast Bestow Curse three times per day as a spell-like ability.

##### Curse Conduit
At 20th level, you become immune to negative energy damage and spells with the curse descriptor. Whenever you confirm a critical hit, you can automatically target the enemy with a Bestow Curse spell-like ability with a random curse effect.

##### Playing a Bloodrager
A Bloodrager has a lot going for it: full BAB, on-tap boost to lethality, bloodline powers and enough spellcasting for useful buffs like *Shield* and *Mirror Image*. Choice of bloodline determines what sort of experience you're going to have.

The outstanding feature of the Abyssal bloodline is the free Enlarge Person while raging. Combine this with a reach weapon and you can largely ignore the penalty from Abyssal Rage which is severe. The attack and damage bonuses mean that Rage keeps ahead of, say, Greater Heroism.

The Arcane bloodline is extremely powerful. Eventually, you can have, for example, *Blur*, *Haste* and *Transformation* cast on yourself when you enter rage which will be every battle when you unlock Limitless Rage. Disrupting enemy casters is gravy.

Angelic Attacks from the Celestial bloodline will give you a decent bonus against most enemies that matter - being able to ignore the DR of powerful enemies is as good as a +10 bonus to damage. You get nice-looking wings as well, rather than the ugly bat-like wings that other bloodlines give you.

The Fey bloodline makes you wait for any significant benefits, but automatic Haste and bane weapons are quite good.

Some of the Hag bloodline abilities come with a saving throw which enemies are likely to make because the DC won't be high. *Dominate Monster* as a bloodline spell seems outstanding but, given that it has the DC of a 4th level spell, you are unlikely to see much benefit from it. The list of bloodline feats is decent but other bloodline abilities are mediocre at best.

The Serpentine bloodline gives you a large AC bonus that stacks with most sources. Typically, most transformations weaken you but the Hydra form can be extremely lethal against enemies which aren't poison-immune. Extra reach is always useful and eventually you become immune to attacks of opportunity which is outstanding.

A Bloodrider gives up useful abilities (Fast Movement and Uncanny Dodge) in exchange for a horse. Note that Gnomes and Halflings must choose a horse as well, even though they can ride medium-sized animals. You get the mount at 5th level which puts you at a disadvantage in the early game and you will need to take Boon Companion to bring your companion up to scratch.

A Greenrager is strictly better than the base class because the ability to ignore difficult terrain is better than a bonus to saves against friendly fire; after all, friendly fire is simply a case of "don't do that". The pathetic summoning abilities (even with +8 Strength, a Dire Boar isn't swinging a 20th level fight) *should* cost the 6th level bloodline feat but at the time of writing (early 2023), they don't.

A Hag-Riven doesn't have to take the Hag bloodline but is not a great choice even so. The main problem is that the claws are natural attacks which will be eclipsed fairly quickly by regular weapons because they don't benefit from iterative attacks (and have bad critical hit parameters to boot). This means that you're trading Fast Movement, Uncanny Dodge and Damage Reduction for a natural armour bonus.

A Mixed-Blood Rager gives up a lot. Not receiving bloodline feats makes them quite feat-starved. However, a combination of, say, Arcane and Serpentine bloodlines would be very good. Note that a Mixed-Blood Rager cannot take the Second Bloodline mythic ability which means that any other Bloodrager can pull off the same trick *and* get bloodline feats and spellcasting.

If a Greenrager is strictly better than the base class, a Primalist is much better. Every bloodline gives you one or more crappy abilities at various levels. Being able to swap these for useful Rage Powers is both cake and eating cake. For example, the Celestial Bloodline gives you great abilities at 1st, 12th and 20th level and nothing much at 4th, 8th and 16th levels. Being able to take the Beast Totem and Lethal Stance lines of Rage Powers instead of mediocre resistances and redundant defensive bonuses is fantastic.

The Reformed Fiend is an exception to the seeming rule that race-specific archetypes have to be garbage. The Abyssal and Infernal bloodlines aren't the best but in *this* game, Hatred of Evil is pretty good.

A Spell Eater is not very good. While not getting hit is better than damage reduction, damage reduction is better than regeneration. It is better not to take damage in the first place than heal damage inflicted. Against a single enemy, the Spelleater's fast healing is more or less equal to the base class's damage reduction. When facing two enemies or more, it becomes greatly inferior. You also lose Uncanny Dodge for minor self-healing that consumes your spells. Needless to say, this is a terrible trade.

A Steelblood trades Uncanny Dodge for the ability to wear heavy armour. You also get Armor Training which makes heavy armour much better - getting +6 AC from Dexterity is way better than +2 AC. However, a fighter does heavy armour better and I prefer the base Bloodrager.
